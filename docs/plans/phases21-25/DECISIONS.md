# Phases 21–25 decisions

These answers are Nathan's October 13, 2026 corrections. They replace earlier
recommendations that conflict with them. CONFLICT-029 records the roadmap
difference. The roadmap PDF is not rewritten.

## Q01 — Growth outcome

Ann manages more properties with less fragmented coordination. Faruk retains
authority and current insight. Filling vacancies is not the principal case.
Only the five operational oversight areas in OWNER_OVERSIGHT.md are built here.

## Q02 — Caller experience

Ann is the normal destination. Routing is Ann-first, with an optional short
keypad branch and an explicit emergency path. There is no voice bot. Choosing
or buying a published number is a later activation decision.

## Q03 — Unanswered calls

An owned callback is created from metadata. Ordinary calls follow the business
calendar. Emergencies follow the accepted on-call, backup, and Faruk path.
This bundle does not offer recorded voicemail. A callback request and a
permitted written path are the alternatives.

## Q04 — Call retention

Store metadata, durations, and staff notes. Do not store audio, transcripts,
speech analysis, or recording-based automation. A connected carrier call does
not mean a person accepted a maintenance case.

## Q05 — Scheduling

Ann controls assignments and appointment commitments. Technicians see an ordered
day. Diagnostic and emergency visits may proceed when their purpose is explicit.
Workers may propose a change with a reason. Phase 22 delivers this.

## Q06 — Materials

Technicians specify the need. Ann coordinates authorized purchasing. A named
person owns ordering and pickup. Technicians verify delivery. Requested,
approved, ordered, received, used, and returned quantities stay distinct.
A substitution keeps the original recommendation. Phase 22 extends Phase 20.

## Q07 — Vendors

Outside help is considered for skill, capacity, deadline, or repeat failure.
Ann chooses an eligible vendor under the approval policy. Comparison includes
scope, timing, qualifications, estimate, warranty, and prior outcomes. Lowest
price does not select the vendor. Phase 22 delivers the workspace. Payment
stays out.

## Q08 — Automated communications

Only factual messages from approved templates and recorded events may send.
Discretionary promises and sensitive housing, financial, or legal content stay
drafts. This bundle does not automatically serve legally consequential notices.
Phase 23 delivers this.

## Q09 — Commitments

Staff turn a written message or a manual call note into a task with an owner
and a due time. A person confirms it. Nothing is inferred from a transcript.
Phase 21 stores the callback on the call. Phase 23 links the shared commitment.

## Q10 — Project approvals

Faruk may approve one defined project: scope, ceiling, execution role, permitted
changes, and expiry. Ann executes that grant. Material changes return for review.
This is not a higher standing limit. Phase 24 delivers this. The ordinary
$1,200 ceiling is not raised.

## Q11 — Absence

Delegation is explicit, scoped, and time-limited, with a named backup. Owner
authority does not transfer by silence. Nathan's technical role does not gain
business approval authority. Phase 24 delivers this.

## Q12 — Budgets

Property and category operating budgets are separate from turnover and project
budgets. Show approved, outstanding commitment, verified cost, remaining budget,
and proposed changes. Remaining budget is not cash. Phase 25 delivers this.

## Q13 — Purchasing

Two comparable estimates are the ordinary discipline for substantial planned
work when practical. A reasoned single-source or emergency decision is allowed.
Match scope, receipt, and invoice. Approving an invoice does not move money.
Phase 25 delivers local records only.

## Q14 — Turns

One plan has owners and dependencies from inspection through readiness. The
purpose is operating capacity. It is not a claim about filling vacancies.
Phase 25 delivers this. Existing leasing behavior stays.

## Q15 — Documents

A permissioned register has an owner, version, validity, reminders, and a
verification step. Uploading a replacement is not verification. Faruk sees
consequential risks. Ann handles ordinary follow-up. Phase 25 delivers this.

## Smaller defaults

- Unknown callers can request a callback. Caller ID is not authentication.
- Repeat contacts stay separate and can point at an existing case.
- A missing provider event stays unresolved. Duration is not invented.
- Written templates can name a language. Original text is kept. No speech transcription.
- Assignment changes record a reason and wait for acceptance.
- Repeat repairs keep the earlier completion and link the new report.
- A no-access visit is an attempt, not a completed repair.
- Templates must be confirmed against the actual equipment.
- Staff replies show ownership and a collision warning.
- Closing a conversation does not close the repair, dispute, or approval.
- Channel preferences, opt-out, and quiet hours are enforced at send time.
- A material revision needs a new decision.
- Unauthorized spending stays an exception. It is not backdated approval.
- Disputed responsibility keeps each account and an attributable resolution.
- Partial delivery keeps received, accepted, rejected, outstanding, used, and returned quantities.
- Document reminders start at 90, 60, 30, and 7 days only where that date applies.
- Expired vendor evidence blocks new assignment and keeps the old record.
- The five oversight areas extend the existing owner briefing.
- Broader analytics are exported for Nathan. Operational views show facts.
- Demonstration scenarios connect calls, maintenance, messages, authority, budget, invoices, and documents.
